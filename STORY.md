## How the book uses this

Origin is curiosity, not a product brief. The day it flew decent, it became a project. It was for Mat first, and for flying at home. Then it was for people who cannot install a sim and cannot buy one. The name VelociDrone is his, said as the sim people could not buy. The comic does not rank it, score it, or say it flies worse.

The school is not named. Children are not drawn. The sentence about school kids is motive, told in words, not a picture of a classroom.

Testers may be named the way the credits page names them: Asylum, Jannes, LeStar, CrapShack, and the RaceGOW designers on that same page. Any other handle that is already printed on webfpv.org or in the simulator may appear. A handle that is not on the public web is blurred.

Partners that the live site already names may be in a picture of that page, because he said anything in the game is fair and the site already shows them. Prices, Patreon tiers, and the maps-only mark that the partners page does not list stay out of the lettering. The task's rule on pricing still stands over "nothing is off limits."

The whoop feeling different is the thing that nearly stopped the work. The book may show the room and ask the question. It may not invent a mass. The craft on screen is the 65 mm whoop. The plant under it, at commit cbaee3f, is the five inch.

Proudest work is the builder: people make their own game.

The last page does not close the story. It points at the sim and leaves the next chapter blank on purpose.

Protagonist is Mat, andAgainFPV, a pilot since 2020. That year is his answer, not a commit. No reference photograph was supplied, so the book does not draw his face. Flight is from the goggles. The name is in the lettering.

The repo is Mathew-Harvey/webFPVPatchNotes, public. The comic is not offered for someone else to republish as if they were him. The reader is published from /docs on GitHub Pages.
