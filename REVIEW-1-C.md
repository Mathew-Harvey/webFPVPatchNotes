# Review 1 C

Product review of chapter 1, The gate. Slice reviewed: the cover and pages 1 to 3 in `docs/index.html`, plus the desktop cover, spread, and end captures and the phone cover, page 1, page 2, and page 3 captures. The book was not edited.

## Verdict

Pass. This is a readable flight comic. The last page offers one way to fly. The empty Next panel is present. A reader can finish wanting a chapter that is not drawn yet.

## Must-fix

None.

## What the reader gets

The cover is the gate, close, at 10 m, filling the glass. WEBFPV and andAgainFPV are the only cover lettering. Nobody speaks. No face. No children.

Page 1 is three beats and no balloons. The gate is small at 14 m and 8 km/h. It is larger at 12 m and 12 km/h. The third panel is low and fast, 10 m, 30 km/h, 0.3 m. The opening grows and the ground comes up. That is the flight.

Page 2 is the miss. The gate edge sits at the bottom of a wide panel. The horizon tips. The last panel is stopped on the grass, the gate still ahead, with the only balloon in the chapter: "Too fast." There is no lesson line under it.

Page 3 prints the calm 14 m approach again, then an empty ruled panel labelled Next, then a mint FLY control. In the chapter data, FLY goes to https://webfpv.org/sim/ and its accessible name is Fly WebFPV. The same ending is on the desktop end capture and the phone page 3 capture. The chrome Next control is disabled there, so FLY is the only live way forward. The noscript copy offers the same link. There is no closing caption that restates a lesson.

On a phone the order is cover, page 1, page 2, page 3. That path is unambiguous.

## Notes

On a wide screen the spread puts page 1 on the right and page 2 on the left. That matches the book: right page first, then the left page. Back, Next, and the arrow keys still move like an English book, left for back and right for forward. A reader who starts on the left of that spread gets the miss before the approach. The footer says "page 1 and page 2" and does not say which side is which. Folios are small. The phone order does not have this problem. This is a clarity risk, not a missing beat. The flight is intact if the right page is read first.

The glass talks more than the story does. Lap, gate 1 of 25, no record yet, pack volts, and the stick labels are all still in frame. The flight still reads, because the gate changes size and the speed steps 8, 12, 30 are the sentence. On the phone, the low panel clips the right edge, so 30 km/h and the height line are cut. The dive still reads from the horizon and the speed lines.

Page 3 uses the same frame as the first panel of page 1. That is the invitation, and it works next to the blank Next panel. A reader in a hurry could think the book jumped backward. The folio and "The gate, page 3" correct that. Nothing else should be added to explain it.

The last desktop view also has a blank endpaper on the left. That is not the Next panel. The Next panel is the ruled box on page 3, with the word Next and FLY in the corner. The dead chrome Next and the blank panel are two different things. Keeping the button dead is right until a chapter exists. FLY is small on that wide page and large on the phone. Mint on the button rhymes with the gate, which helps it read as the way to fly.

The phone cover leaves a tall band of sky above the gate. The gate is still the subject and still close. A faint weight label shows through the cover glow. It is leftover glass, not part of the sentence.
